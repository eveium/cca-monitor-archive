Overview of Policy 013: MCE's Reserve Policy
Rate setting & PCIA · information only · priority 2/5 · packet
Staff presents comparison of MCE's liquidity position and reserve targets against peer CCAs and public agencies to support board discussion on whether Policy 013 should be updated. Current policy targets 240 days cash on hand; MCE ended FYE 2026 at 250 days, above target. Staff evaluates whether policy should be simplified to focus exclusively on Days Liquidity on Hand (DLOH) rather than reserves percentage.
Why it matters: Reserve policy and liquidity targets affect MCE's rate stability, financial flexibility, and ability to manage energy cost volatility—factors relevant to customer rates and competitiveness.
Priority rationale: Policy review is routine governance; no immediate rate or operational changes proposed, but reserve levels do support long-term financial stability.
Proposed Amendment of Policy 014: MCE's Investment Policy
Rate setting & PCIA · proposed · priority 2/5 · packet
Staff proposes targeted amendments to MCE's Investment Policy to incorporate recent California Government Code changes, expand diversification opportunities, and improve policy clarity. Key changes include extended maturity limits for U.S. Treasuries and federal agency securities (up to 7 years), increased commercial paper maturity (to 397 days), addition of bankers' acceptances, expanded municipal securities eligibility, and new LGIP authorization.
Why it matters: Investment policy changes affect MCE's ability to optimize returns on reserves and invested funds, which supports financial stability and long-term rate competitiveness.
Priority rationale: Technical investment policy updates improve portfolio management flexibility and alignment with peer practices, but do not directly impact near-term rates or procurement.
