California Community Choice Association's Comments on the Proposed Decision Resolving Track Two Issues (R.25-02-005)
Rate setting & PCIA · information only · priority 5/5 · packet
CalCCA files extensive comments opposing the Proposed Decision on pre-2019 banked renewable energy credits (RECs), arguing that the decision violates section 366.2(g)'s requirement to account for benefits that remain with bundled customers. CalCCA contends that customers who paid for banked RECs while bundled but later departed for CCA service should receive their share of compliance benefits when those RECs are used by IOUs.
Why it matters: This is a major PCIA litigation involving the allocation of renewable energy benefits between bundled and unbundled customers and the potential cost shifts between them under California's power charge indifference framework.
Priority rationale: This Track 2 decision concerns the statutory obligation to prevent cost shifts between bundled and unbundled customers on an issue that has been litigated for six years; addresses a fundamental interpretation of section 366.2(g) indifference requirements.
Joint Proposal of Southern California Edison Company and California Community Choice Association for Changes to Climate Credit (R.25-07-013)
Equity & low-income programs · proposed · priority 3/5 · packet
SCE and CalCCA jointly propose either maintaining the status quo for the California Climate Credit (equal flat credit to all residential customers) or, if changes are warranted, providing an enhanced credit of up to 2x for CARE/FERA customers. The proposal is designed to balance affordability, simplicity, and implementation feasibility.
Why it matters: This proposal addresses how greenhouse gas allowance revenues are distributed to residential customers, with direct impact on low-income customer affordability through the Climate Credit program.
Priority rationale: Relevant to equity and low-income affordability programs, though the joint proposal represents consensus between CCA and IOU rather than contentious advocacy.
California Community Choice Association's Comments on the IEPR Commissioner Workshop on Energy Demand Forecast Inputs and Assumptions (26-IEPR-03)
Renewable procurement & PPAs · information only · priority 3/5 · packet
CalCCA comments on the California Energy Commission's Integrated Energy Policy Report demand forecast, recommending continued engagement with CCAs on large load development, a formal process for allocating Resource Adequacy obligations for data center load, and continued exclusion of known loads from procurement scenarios until certainty improves.
Why it matters: Addresses how demand forecasting and large load (especially data center) integration affects resource planning and procurement obligations for load-serving entities including CCAs; relates to IRP and Resource Adequacy compliance.
Priority rationale: Addresses uncertainty in demand forecasting and its impact on resource procurement and RA obligations, which has implications for renewable procurement planning and cost allocation.
CPUC Energy Division Draft 2028 RA LOLE Study Results Workshop (R.25-10-003 Track 2)
State regulatory proceedings · information only · priority 4/5 · packet
CPUC staff presents updated Loss of Load Expectation (LOLE) studies and Planning Reserve Margin (PRM) recommendations for 2028 and 2029 Resource Adequacy compliance years, including corrected modeling, sensitivity analyses, and multiple calibration methodologies.
Why it matters: This RA reliability study directly informs the Resource Adequacy procurement requirements that apply to all load-serving entities including CCAs; findings affect PRM compliance obligations and procurement costs.
Priority rationale: Critical to RA compliance and procurement obligations; LOLE study results directly determine reserve margin requirements and resource adequacy procurement needs for all LSEs.
